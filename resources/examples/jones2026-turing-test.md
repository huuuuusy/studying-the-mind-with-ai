# A language model passes a three-party Turing test, with a persona

**Study.** Jones, C. R., & Bergen, B. K. (2026). Large language models pass a standard three-party Turing test. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 123*(21), e2524472123. [doi:10.1073/pnas.2524472123](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2524472123) · [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.23674) (earlier arXiv version, 2025: *Large language models pass the Turing test*)

**In the talk.** The opening, [Start here](../../docs/start-here.md): one real game from this test, in which the judge took the AI for the human. The talk's conclusion: being picked as human tells us about the judges' cues, not yet about the machine's mind. Testing the machine also tested the judges.

**Question.** In Turing's original three-party design, can people tell a machine from a human when they talk to both at once?

**Method.** Two randomised, controlled and preregistered Turing tests were run on independent populations. Each interrogator (the judge) held five-minute text conversations simultaneously with a human participant and with one of four systems: ELIZA, GPT-4o, LLaMa-3.1-405B or GPT-4.5. They then judged which partner was human. Some models received a prompt to adopt a humanlike persona and some did not. A third study used 15-minute games.

**Finding.**
- With the persona prompt, GPT-4.5 was judged to be the human 73% of the time, significantly more often than the real human. LLaMa-3.1 was judged human 56% of the time, not significantly different from the humans it was compared with.
- **Without the persona prompt, the same models did significantly worse (GPT-4.5 36%, LLaMa-3.1 38%)** and did not consistently beat the baselines, ELIZA and GPT-4o (23% and 21%).
- In 15-minute games, two persona-prompted models reached 56% and 59%.
- Interrogators relied more on style and social–emotional cues than on traditional notions of intelligence.

**Why read it.** It is the cleanest modern blind rating. Its most instructive result is the contrast with and without the persona: the outcome depends on a condition the experimenters set.

**Limits.** Passing shows that the conversation *looks* human to these interrogators in a short exchange. It does not show what, if anything, lies behind it. The authors stress that the test measures how people tell humans from machines.

**Data and code.** The studies were preregistered; see the article for data and materials.
