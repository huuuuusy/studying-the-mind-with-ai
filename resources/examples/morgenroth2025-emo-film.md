# Forty-four raters, fourteen films: Emo-FilM

**Study.** Morgenroth, E., Moia, S., Vilaclara, L., Fournier, R., Muszynski, M., Ploumitsakou, M., Almató-Bellavista, M., Vuilleumier, P., & Van De Ville, D. (2025). Emo-FilM: A multimodal dataset for affective neuroscience using naturalistic stimuli. *Scientific Data, 12*, 684. [doi:10.1038/s41597-025-04803-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-025-04803-5) (open access)

**In the talk.** Not shown; read it with Part 2, [A group: people as the answer](../../docs/part2-people-as-the-answer.md): observers agree on what happened, not on what it means.

**Question.** How do people's emotional responses unfold while they watch films, and how much do raters agree when they describe them?

**Method.** Raters annotated 14 short films lasting more than two and a half hours in total. There were 44 raters and 50 items: discrete emotions, plus components of emotion from appraisal, motivation, motor expression, physiological response and feeling. A separate sample of 30 people watched the same films during functional MRI, with heart rate, respiration and electrodermal activity recorded.

**Finding.** The ratings had a mean inter-rater agreement of 0.38. That is moderate at best, for a task in which every rater saw the same films. The data set pairs these ratings with brain and body measurements of people watching the same films.

**Why read it.** It is a public, carefully documented example of a human reference that disagrees. A model that rates emotion in these films should be judged against this spread, not against one rater's or the average rater's ratings.

**Limits.** The data are designed for affective neuroscience. The agreement figure is a mean; agreement on single items and moments can be higher or lower.

**Data and code.** On OpenNeuro (data sets ds004872 and ds004892), released under CC0.
