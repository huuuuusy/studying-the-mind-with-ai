# What language models can and cannot do for psychology

**Study.** Demszky, D., Yang, D., Yeager, D. S., Bryan, C. J., Clapper, M., Chandhok, S., Eichstaedt, J. C., Hecht, C., Jamieson, J., Johnson, M., Jones, M., Krettek-Cobb, D., Lai, L., JonesMitchell, N., Ong, D. C., Dweck, C. S., Gross, J. J., & Pennebaker, J. W. (2023). Using large language models in psychology. *Nature Reviews Psychology, 2*, 688–701. [doi:10.1038/s44159-023-00241-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44159-023-00241-5) · [author manuscript (NSF repository)](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10575508)

**In the talk.** Not shown; read it with [Part 4, AI in your own research](../../docs/part4-ai-in-your-research.md): a map of where language models fit in psychological research, and where they do not yet.

**Question.** Language data are central to every area of psychology. What do large language models change, and what are they not yet ready for?

**Method.** A Perspective article by psychologists and computer scientists. It explains how language models are built and why that lets them produce human-like language without thinking or feeling like a person. It then reviews their uses in psychological measurement, experimentation and practice.

**Finding.** The authors argue that language models could advance psychological measurement, experimentation and practice, but that they are not yet ready for many of the most transformative applications. They examine four major concerns and how each might be overcome. They recommend three investments: field-initiated "keystone" data sets, more standardised performance benchmarks (shared tests for comparing models), and shared computing and analysis infrastructure, so that research with these models is equitable.

**Why read it.** It is the broadest single map of the topic written for psychologists, with authors from across the field. It is a good first reading before any project in this part.

**Limits.** It is a Perspective from 2023: the models have changed since, though the concerns it raises have not gone away.

**Data and code.** None; it is a review and argument.
