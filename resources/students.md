# For psychology students: reading list by area

*给心理学学生：按方向的阅读清单。本页的文献条目保留英文；中文导读从[这里](../docs/start-here.zh.md)开始。*

Companion to the talk *Studying the Human Mind with AI: From Behavioral Analysis to Role Simulation* (Shiyu Hu, Wenzhou-Kean University, November 2026).

The talk asks one question: **it looks capable; how would we know it really is?** It asks four questions, each a little bigger than the last (one person, a group, a society, and your own research), each with a check borrowed from psychology: right answer, right reason? (compare the mistakes, not only the scores); whose standard does it follow? (compare with a group, not one person); is it like us enough to study us? (compare it with real people); and how can you use it? (check it first, then use it). The guides in this repository follow the same four parts.

This page is organised by area of psychology; the [main list](README.md) follows the parts of the talk. Each entry is marked **classic** or **frontier**, with one line on why it matters. Links only; every link was checked on 2026-10-13 (additions 2026-10-20). For working with AI researchers, see [working-together.md](working-together.md).

## Start here (four readings)

*从这里开始（四篇）*

- **classic** · Pfungst, O. (1911). *Clever Hans (the horse of Mr. von Osten).* [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/33936). A horse that seemed to count was reading tiny, unintended movements of its questioner's head. The first control experiment on a "too clever" answer.
- **classic** · Turing, A. M. (1950). Computing machinery and intelligence. *Mind.* [doi:10.1093/mind/lix.236.433](https://doi.org/10.1093/mind/lix.236.433). The imitation game: judging a machine by whether people can tell it apart.
- **frontier** · Demszky, D., et al. (2023). Using large language models in psychology. *Nature Reviews Psychology.* [doi:10.1038/s44159-023-00241-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44159-023-00241-5). A broad map of what language models can and cannot do for psychology.
- **frontier** · Geirhos, R., et al. (2020). Shortcut learning in deep neural networks. *Nature Machine Intelligence.* [doi:10.1038/s42256-020-00257-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-020-00257-z). Why a right answer can come from the wrong cue.

## Cognitive psychology and neuroscience

*认知心理学与神经科学*

- **classic** · Hubel, D. H., & Wiesel, T. N. (1959). Receptive fields of single neurones in the cat's striate cortex. *Journal of Physiology.* [doi:10.1113/jphysiol.1959.sp006308](https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.1959.sp006308). And (1962), [doi:10.1113/jphysiol.1962.sp006837](https://doi.org/10.1113/jphysiol.1962.sp006837). The visual cortex as layers of simple and complex cells.
- **classic** · Fukushima, K. (1980). Neocognitron. *Biological Cybernetics.* [doi:10.1007/bf00344251](https://doi.org/10.1007/bf00344251). Image AI built on Hubel and Wiesel's cells.
- **classic** · Thorndike, E. L. (1898). Animal intelligence. *Psychological Review Monograph Supplements.* [doi:10.1037/h0092987](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0092987). Learning by reward, which reinforcement learning later borrowed.
- **classic** · Sutton, R. S., & Barto, A. G. (2018). *Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction* (2nd ed.). [Authors' book page](http://incompleteideas.net/book/the-book-2nd.html). Section 1.7 traces the field to animal learning.
- **frontier** · Mnih, V., et al. (2015). Human-level control through deep reinforcement learning. *Nature.* [doi:10.1038/nature14236](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14236).
- **frontier** · Christiano, P., et al. (2017). Deep reinforcement learning from human preferences. *NeurIPS.* [Proceedings](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/2017/hash/d5e2c0adad503c91f91df240d0cd4e49-Abstract.html). And Ouyang, L., et al. (2022). Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback. *NeurIPS.* [doi:10.52202/068431-2011](https://doi.org/10.52202/068431-2011). How human judgements now train chat assistants.
- **frontier** · Lapuschkin, S., et al. (2019). Unmasking Clever Hans predictors and assessing what machines really learn. *Nature Communications.* [doi:10.1038/s41467-019-08987-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-08987-4).
- **frontier** · Geirhos, R., Meding, K., & Wichmann, F. A. (2020). Beyond accuracy: Quantifying trial-by-trial behaviour of CNNs and humans by measuring error consistency. *NeurIPS.* [Proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/9f6992966d4c363ea0162a056cb45fe5-Abstract.html). Compare errors trial by trial, not just scores.
- **frontier** · Geirhos, R., et al. (2021). Partial success in closing the gap between human and machine vision. *NeurIPS.* [Proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/c8877cff22082a16395a57e97232bb6f-Abstract.html).
- **frontier** · Yamins, D. L. K., et al. (2014). Performance-optimized hierarchical models predict neural responses in higher visual cortex. *PNAS.* [doi:10.1073/pnas.1403112111](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1403112111).
- **frontier** · Binz, M., & Schulz, E. (2023). Using cognitive psychology to understand GPT-3. *PNAS.* [doi:10.1073/pnas.2218523120](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2218523120).
- **frontier** · Binz, M., et al. (2025). A foundation model to predict and capture human cognition. *Nature.* [doi:10.1038/s41586-025-09215-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09215-4). And the reply by Orr, M., et al. (2025). Not even wrong: On the limits of prediction as explanation in cognitive science. [arXiv:2510.03311](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.03311).
- **frontier** · Jones, C. R., & Bergen, B. K. (2026). Large language models pass a standard three-party Turing test. *PNAS.* [doi:10.1073/pnas.2524472123](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2524472123).
- **frontier** · Center for AI Safety, Scale AI & HLE Contributors Consortium (2026). A benchmark of expert-level academic questions to assess AI capabilities. *Nature.* [doi:10.1038/s41586-025-09962-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09962-4).
- **frontier** · Zhaoping, L. (2023). Peripheral and central sensation: Multisensory orienting and recognition across species. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences.* [doi:10.1016/j.tics.2023.03.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2023.03.001). A theory of "where to look" and "what it is" that guided one of our trackers.
- **frontier** · Hu, S., Zhao, X., Huang, L., & Huang, K. (2023). Global instance tracking: Locating target more like humans. *IEEE TPAMI.* [doi:10.1109/tpami.2022.3153312](https://doi.org/10.1109/tpami.2022.3153312). People and trackers on the same videos.
- **frontier** · Hu, S., Zhao, X., & Huang, K. (2024). SOTVerse: A user-defined task space of single object tracking. *IJCV.* [doi:10.1007/s11263-023-01908-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11263-023-01908-5).
- **frontier** · Zhang, D., Hu, S., et al. (2024). Beyond accuracy: Tracking more like human via visual search. *NeurIPS.* [doi:10.52202/079017-0086](https://doi.org/10.52202/079017-0086). Close scores, different mistakes.
- **frontier** · Li, X., Li, X., Hu, S., Huang, K., & Zhang, W. (2026). CausalStep: A benchmark for explicit stepwise causal reasoning in videos. *AAAI.* [doi:10.1609/aaai.v40i8.37582](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v40i8.37582). Describing is not reasoning.

## Measurement and psychometrics

*测量与心理测量*

- **classic** · Thurstone, L. L. (1927). A law of comparative judgment. *Psychological Review.* [doi:10.1037/h0070288](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0070288). Ask people to compare two things at a time and turn the choices into a scale: the model behind how chat assistants are trained and ranked today.
- **classic** · Loftus, E. F., & Palmer, J. C. (1974). Reconstruction of automobile destruction. *Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior.* [doi:10.1016/s0022-5371(74)80011-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0022-5371(74)80011-3). One word in a question changes what people report.
- **frontier** · Gilardi, F., Alizadeh, M., & Kubli, M. (2023). ChatGPT outperforms crowd workers for text-annotation tasks. *PNAS.* [doi:10.1073/pnas.2305016120](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2305016120). AI as a coder: agreement with itself is not accuracy.
- **frontier** · Serapio-García, G., et al. (2025). A psychometric framework for evaluating and shaping personality traits in large language models. *Nature Machine Intelligence.* [doi:10.1038/s42256-025-01115-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-025-01115-6). A persona is a manipulation; check that it moved the trait.
- **frontier** · Wang, A., Morgenstern, J., & Dickerson, J. P. (2025). Large language models that replace human participants can harmfully misportray and flatten identity groups. *Nature Machine Intelligence.* [doi:10.1038/s42256-025-00986-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-025-00986-z).
- **frontier** · Li, X., Li, X., Hu, S., Guo, Y., & Zhang, W. (2026). VerifyBench: A systematic benchmark for evaluating reasoning verifiers across domains. *AAAI.* [doi:10.1609/aaai.v40i38.40448](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v40i38.40448). Check the checker before it judges.

## Emotion and affective science

*情绪与情感科学*

- **classic** · Gross, J. J. (1998). The emerging field of emotion regulation: An integrative review. *Review of General Psychology.* [doi:10.1037/1089-2680.2.3.271](https://doi.org/10.1037/1089-2680.2.3.271). The process model behind reappraisal; read it before judging what an AI "reappraisal" actually does.
- **frontier** · Barrett, L. F., Adolphs, R., Marsella, S., Martinez, A. M., & Pollak, S. D. (2019). Emotional expressions reconsidered: Challenges to inferring emotion from human facial movements. *Psychological Science in the Public Interest.* [doi:10.1177/1529100619832930](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100619832930). A face is not a readout of feeling, so a machine that "reads emotion" needs a better reference than one label.
- **frontier** · Schlegel, K., Sommer, N. R., & Mortillaro, M. (2025). Large language models are proficient in solving and creating emotional intelligence tests. *Communications Psychology.* [doi:10.1038/s44271-025-00258-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44271-025-00258-x). Models beat the human norms on written emotion tests; ask what the items let them respond to.
- **frontier** · Zhan, H., Zheng, A., Lee, Y. K., Suh, J., Li, J. J., & Ong, D. C. (2024). Large language models are capable of offering cognitive reappraisal, if guided. *COLM.* [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=yK8MT91dQY). Emotion-regulation principles written into the instructions, with clinical psychologists as judges: theory in, expert check out.
- **frontier** · Morgenroth, E., et al. (2025). Emo-FilM: A multimodal dataset for affective neuroscience using naturalistic stimuli. *Scientific Data.* [doi:10.1038/s41597-025-04803-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-025-04803-5). Data: [OpenNeuro](https://openneuro.org/datasets/ds004872). People rated 50 emotion items moment by moment while watching films and agreed only moderately; that spread is the yardstick for any AI emotion reading.

## Using this list

*怎样使用这份清单*

- Read one **classic** and one **frontier** entry from your own area first; the classic usually shows the method the frontier paper needs.
- For each AI result, ask three questions: what is it responding to, what is it compared with, and which way does the inference run?
- Corrections and suggestions are welcome.
